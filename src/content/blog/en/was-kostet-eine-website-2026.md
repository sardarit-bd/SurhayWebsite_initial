"What does a website cost?" is the most common question in a first consultation — and the honest answer is: it depends on what it's supposed to achieve. That sounds evasive, but it's the heart of the matter. A website isn't an off-the-shelf product; it's a tool with a job to do.

## The three price brackets — and what you get

**Under €1,000: site builders.** Wix, Squarespace and the like are legitimate for getting started. You pay with limited performance, interchangeable design and a ceiling on everything that comes later: SEO, extensions, speed.

**€2,500–8,000: custom-built by a professional.** Most SME projects live in this range. Custom design, clean code, a CMS for self-service editing, an SEO foundation. This is where it's decided whether your website is a cost center or a sales channel.

**€10,000+: complex requirements.** Online shops with inventory systems, portals, custom web applications. If you receive quotes like this for a five-page website, ask for the reasoning.

## How to recognize a fair quote

A serious quote answers three questions: What exactly will be delivered? What does it cost — as a fixed price? And what happens after launch? Be wary of hourly estimates without a cap, of "SEO included" without specifics, and of agencies that talk design before they know your goals.

## The real question: what does a bad website cost?

Quick math: if your website brings in just two more qualified leads per month and your average order value is €2,000, a €6,000 website pays for itself in six weeks. The most expensive website is the one that achieves nothing — no matter how little it cost.
