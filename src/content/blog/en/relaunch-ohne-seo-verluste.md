The classic relaunch accident goes like this: the new website goes live, everyone celebrates the design — and four weeks later organic traffic has collapsed by 60 percent. Google can't find the old pages anymore and doesn't know the new ones yet. The insidious part: the damage only shows with a delay, when the project budget is long spent.

## Before the relaunch: take inventory

- **Capture all indexed URLs** — via Google Search Console and a crawling tool. You can only redirect what you know about.
- **Identify your top pages:** which URLs bring traffic, rankings, backlinks? These pages are your capital and deserve special care.
- **Document existing rankings** so you can compare after the relaunch instead of guessing.

## During the relaunch: the non-negotiables

- **301 redirects for every old URL** to its new counterpart — individually, not wholesale to the homepage. The blanket redirect is the most common and most expensive mistake.
- **Carry over title tags and meta descriptions** or improve them deliberately — don't let the CMS regenerate them.
- **Check internal links:** no links to old URLs, no redirect chains.
- **Resubmit your sitemap.xml** and watch Search Console for crawl errors.

## After the relaunch: two weeks on watch

The first 14 days decide everything. Check Search Console daily for 404 errors and indexing issues. Individual ranking fluctuations are normal and settle down; wholesale ranking losses on important pages are an alarm signal — and still fixable now, while Google hasn't forgotten the old signals.

A properly planned relaunch loses no rankings. In most of our projects, visibility actually increases — because the new website is faster, better structured and technically cleaner than the old one.
